The governance problem

Capable is not the same as governed.

Your agent can reason, call tools, and talk to customers. That does not mean it follows your rules. The usual fix is more instructions, more retrieved documents, more memory. Behavior gets harder to control, costlier to run, and harder to explain.

Policy lives in documents, not in behavior.

Refund limits, approval chains, and compliance constraints sit in a wiki. The agent sees them only if someone remembered to put them in the prompt.

The model receives everything.

Full profiles and policy libraries bury the one rule that matters under many that don't, and every request pays for the extra tokens.

Nobody can explain the action.

When an agent violates policy, teams have to reconstruct what it saw and why it acted. That does not scale to production review or audit.

What you get

Agents that follow your rules, with proof.

Policies stop being suggestions in a prompt. They become explicit rules with a fixed order of authority: organizational policy, then role norms, then personal preference.

The behavior unit

Meet the Sim.

The rules in that example live in Sims. A Sim is a small, structured set of rules for one context: a company's refund policy, a support role's approval limits, or a person's preference for fast resolution. Sims keep behavior specific, portable, and inspectable.

One person, team, or organization can have many Sims. For each task, VersarAI selects only the relevant ones, then resolves any conflict before the agent acts. The Sims architecture is patent-pending.
